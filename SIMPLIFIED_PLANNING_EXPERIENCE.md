# Simpler, More Visual Planning Experience

Design direction prepared by Yang Yu after reviewing the current four-step shop-planning workflow.

## Problem observed

The workflow contains useful questions, but too many controls are visible at once. Concept feels like a questionnaire, Location asks users to estimate several unfamiliar measures, and Budget exposes both essential numbers and advanced assumptions together. The map and final metrics help, but visual feedback arrives too late.

## Recommended quick path

The default experience should ask only eight essential questions:

1. What kind of shop is it?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why would customers choose it?
4. Where might it open?
5. How much cash is available?
6. What will opening cost?
7. What are the monthly fixed costs?
8. What monthly sales seem realistic?

Margin, representative-product pricing, customer evidence, parking, traffic estimates, spoilage, seasonality, and other assumptions should remain available under clearly labelled optional sections.

## Make each step more visual

- Replace long business-type menus with illustrated choice cards.
- Turn the customer promise into a live `Your idea is taking shape` preview.
- Lead the Location step with the map and nearby partner ecosystem; put manual scoring assumptions under `Adjust assumptions`.
- Show a live money picture after the four core budget figures, with advanced pricing and seasonal controls collapsed by default.
- End with one decision, three plain-language reasons, and one small next experiment.

## Keep three planning depths

- **Quick:** eight answers, approximately three minutes.
- **Guided:** adds useful prompts and explanations for first-time owners.
- **Detailed:** exposes every existing assumption for users who want full control.

All three modes should use the same calculations. Changing depth changes only how much is shown, never the underlying financial logic or evidence boundaries.
